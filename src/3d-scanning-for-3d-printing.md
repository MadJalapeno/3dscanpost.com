# 3D Scanning for 3D Printing

Need to reproduce a broken part, make an adapter that fits an existing object, or print a copy of something that has no CAD model? A 3D scan gives you a digital mesh of the real thing, which you can load into a slicer or modelling tool.

## From physical part to STL

1. **Send photos** of your part from several angles, plus approximate dimensions and material.
2. **We quote and recommend a scan type.** Laser scanning suits precision parts; infrared scanning suits organic or colored objects. See the [comparison](/#scan-types).
3. **Ship your part.** You pay a 50% deposit first.
4. **We scan and send screenshots** for your approval, then you pay the balance.
5. **You receive STL and PLY files**, and your original part is shipped back.

## STL or PLY?

- **STL** is the format nearly every slicer and CAD package accepts. It stores geometry only.
- **PLY** can carry color information, which matters if you're printing in color or want a realistic visual of the object.

We deliver both.

## Are scans print-ready?

Scan files are high-quality meshes, but depending on your use they may need cleanup, hole filling or optimization before printing. Most slicers import STL directly. If you need a part that matches existing geometry precisely, our laser mode has ±0.2mm accuracy, and our [reverse engineering](/reverse-engineering-3d-scanning/) service can recreate the part as a STEP file in Solidworks.

## Tricky surfaces

Shiny, transparent or very dark parts can be hard to scan. We can apply a temporary scanning spray that evaporates after scanning, or use infrared mode, which needs no spray.
