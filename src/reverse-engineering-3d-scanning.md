# Reverse Engineering with 3D Scanning

When the drawings are lost, the supplier is gone, or the part was never modelled, a 3D scan captures its geometry so you can measure it, inspect it or rebuild it.

## Step 1: a precise scan

Our laser scanning mode delivers **±0.2mm accuracy** and works best on mechanical parts and hard surfaces with defined edges. You receive the scan as **<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STL_(file_format)" target="_blank" rel="noopener">STL<span class="sr-only"> (Wikipedia, opens in a new tab)</span><span aria-hidden="true" title="External link, opens in a new tab"> ↗</span></a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLY_(file_format)" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PLY<span class="sr-only"> (Wikipedia, opens in a new tab)</span><span aria-hidden="true" title="External link, opens in a new tab"> ↗</span></a>** meshes. Parts that are reflective, transparent or very dark can be prepared with a temporary scanning spray that evaporates after scanning.

## Step 2 (optional): CAD conversion

A mesh is not an editable CAD model. Turning one into parametric CAD requires understanding design intent, which differs for every application. Without knowing that intent, the best we can do is recreate your part in **SolidWorks** and provide a **<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_10303-21" target="_blank" rel="noopener">STEP<span class="sr-only"> (Wikipedia, opens in a new tab)</span><span aria-hidden="true" title="External link, opens in a new tab"> ↗</span></a> file** suitable for manufacturing.

While there are automatic services that claim to make a STEP file from a 3D scan we've yet to find one that works well with anything beyond simple geometry. When we convert a part to a CAD file we will measure the original part as we build a model in SolidWorks, using the scan as an underlay. We have a granite table, various calibrated measuring tools and 20+ years experience building complex models.

One thing to bear in mind: we're working from a single physical part. Every part is made within a tolerance, so yours may sit at the upper or lower limit. A model built from it matches that part, which isn't necessarily the nominal design dimension.

Scans are provided on a best-effort basis. Check the scan and any recreated model against the physical part before using it for critical applications. Our [service disclaimer](/#contact) has the details.
